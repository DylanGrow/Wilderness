The Survival Mindset

Panic kills more people than the wilderness. Your mind is your most critical tool—or your greatest liability.

The STOP Acronym

When in trouble, apply STOP: Sit/Stop, Think, Observe, Plan.

The Rule of 3s

  • 3 minutes without air: Airway first.
  • 3 hours without shelter in harsh conditions: Hypothermia risk.
  • 3 days without water: Dehydration impairs judgment.
  • 3 weeks without food: Starvation is long-term.

Positive Mental Attitude

Break challenges into small goals. Talk to yourself. Remember: you have survived 100% of your worst days so far.

Decision framework: Does this address my most urgent need? What are risks of inaction? What resources does this consume? Can I reverse this?

The 5 C's

The 5 C's of Survivability framework for prioritizing gear that multiplies your skills.

C1 — Cutting Tool

A blade is essential for shelter, food, fire, tools, first aid. Prefer full-tang fixed blade 3-5". Keep sharp, dry, sheathed. Cut away from body.

C2 — Combustion Device

Fire = warmth, water purification, signaling, morale. Carry: lighter ×2, ferro rod, waterproof matches. Tinder: char cloth, fatwood, birch bark, cattail fluff. Store dry.

Fire Lays

  • Teepee: Best for ignition, tall flame.
  • Log Cabin: Long burn, stable for cooking.
  • Star: Fuel-efficient, adjustable.
  • Lean-to: Wind-resistant.

Lighting steps: 1) Clear site, 2) Gather tinder/kindling/fuel, 3) Build lay, 4) Ignite from upwind, 5) Nurture flame, 6) Scale up. Extinguish completely: stir, water, stir, feel.

C3 — Cover (Shelter)

Shelter priority #2 in cold/wet. Site: windbreak, flat, drain away. Avoid widow makers, flood zones. Insulate with dead air space.

Heat Loss

  • Conduction: Ground contact → insulate.
  • Convection: Wind → windproof layer.
  • Radiation: Heat loss → reflective layer.
  • Evaporation: Moisture → stay dry, ventilate.

Shelter Builds

Debris Hut: Ridgepole 9-10', ribbing 45°, debris 2' thick, bedding 4". Lean-To: Ridgeline 4-5', roof 45-60°, reflector wall. Quinzhee: Snow mound 6-8', sinter 2hrs, depth guides, ventilate. A-Frame Tarp: Ridgeline, drape, stake 45°.

C4 — Container

Single-wall metal for boiling water. Stainless/titanium, 1L+, wide mouth. Improvised: birch bark, large leaves, clay.

C5 — Cordage

Cordage multiplies tools. Natural: dogbane, nettle, inner bark. Paracord 550lb: sheath + 7-9 strands.

Essential Knots

  • Bowline: Fixed loop, rescue.
  • Clove Hitch: Quick post attachment.
  • Prusik: Friction hitch, ascending.
  • Sheet Bend: Join different ropes.
  • Figure-8: Stopper knot.

Finding Water

Dehydration timeline: 1hr: thirst, reduced performance. 3hrs: headache, fatigue. 6hrs: confusion, impaired judgment. 12hrs+: organ stress, collapse risk.

Reading Terrain for Water

  • Follow valleys/drainages downhill.
  • Animal trails converging → water.
  • Green vegetation, willows, cottonwood in arid areas.
  • Insects (bees, flies) within 1 mile of water.
  • Morning dew: collect with cloth, wring into container.
  • Birds in V-formation at dawn/dusk often heading to water.

Water Sources Ranked by Safety

  1. Rainwater (safest, collect in clean container)
  2. Spring source (not downstream)
  3. Moving stream above human activity
  4. Still pond/lake (riskiest—stagnant = pathogens)

Purification Methods

  • Boiling: 1 min at sea level, 3 min above 6500ft. Kills all biological pathogens.
  • Chemical (iodine/chlorine): Effective vs bacteria/viruses, doesn't remove sediment, alters taste.
  • Filtration: Removes particulates, most pathogens; not viruses unless specified.
  • SODIS: Clear PET bottle, 6hrs direct sun—UV + heat disinfect.

Improvised filter: Layer grass → charcoal → sand → gravel in container. Pour water through. Still boil after.

Water from plants: Transpiration bag (branch + bag), green bamboo sections, some cacti (never barrel cactus—can cause vomiting).

Warning signs: Foul odor, unusual color, animal carcasses upstream, industrial runoff. When in doubt, purify.

Daily needs: 2-4L depending on activity, temperature, humidity. Sip regularly; don't wait for thirst.

Finding Your Way

Written Navigation

Compass basics: Baseplate, rotating bezel, magnetic needle (red = north), direction-of-travel arrow.

Magnetic vs True North: Magnetic north moves; declination is the angle difference. Adjust compass or map. Find local declination before travel.

Taking a bearing: 1) Point travel arrow at target. 2) Rotate bezel until orienting arrow aligns with magnetic needle. 3) Read bearing at index line.

Following a bearing: Pick a landmark on your bearing, walk to it, repeat. For obstacles: aim off intentionally, or box around (90° turns, count paces).

Triangulation: Take bearings to two known landmarks. Draw lines on map; intersection = your position.

Sun Navigation

  • Shadow stick: Stick in ground, mark shadow tip, wait 15min, mark again. Line = E-W; perpendicular = N-S.
  • Watch method (analog, N hemisphere): Point hour hand at sun; bisect angle between hour hand and 12 = S.
  • Sun arc: Rises ~E, sets ~W; higher in summer, lower in winter.

Star Navigation

  • Polaris (N Star): Find Big Dipper; follow pointer stars 5× their distance = Polaris = true north.
  • Southern Cross (S hemisphere): Extend long axis 4.5× to find south celestial pole.
  • Star movement: Stars rise E, set W. Face a rising star = facing E.

If lost: STAY PUT unless you have definitive reason to move (visible rescue, known safe route). Moving increases search area.

Written Signaling

Universal distress: 3 of anything = distress signal. 3 shots, 3 fires, 3 whistle blasts, 3 flashes. Pause, repeat.

SOS: ... --- ... Not an abbreviation; simplest, most recognizable pattern.

Ground-to-Air Signals

Stomp in snow or arrange rocks/logs (min. 10ft tall for aircraft visibility):

  • X = Need medical help
  • V = Need help
  • → = Traveling this direction
  • LL = All OK
  • N = No / F = Need food & water

Signal mirror: Aim flash at aircraft by holding mirror near eye, looking through sighting hole at target, adjusting until flash hits target.

Fire signals: 3 fires in triangle. Add green leaves for white smoke (visible against forest), rubber/plastic for black smoke (visible against snow/overcast).

Whistle: 3 blasts, pause, repeat. Carry pealess whistle (Fox 40)—works wet, cold, won't freeze.

Visual signals: Spread bright gear/clothing in open area. Movement attracts attention.

Helicopter signaling: Never run toward helicopter. Arms crossed overhead = "I need help". Thumbs up = "I'm OK". Stay clear of tail rotor.

Wilderness First Aid

Field triage only. Get to professional help ASAP. These protocols stabilize, not cure.

Primary Survey (ABCDE)

  1. Airway: Clear obstructions, tilt head back, lift chin.
  2. Breathing: Look/listen/feel for breath. If none, begin rescue breathing if trained.
  3. Circulation: Check pulse. Control severe bleeding first.
  4. Disability: Assess consciousness (AVPU: Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive).
  5. Exposure: Remove/wet clothing to assess injuries; prevent hypothermia.

Bleeding Control

  • Direct pressure: Firm pressure with clean cloth/gauze. Hold 5-10 min without peeking.
  • Wound packing: For deep wounds, pack gauze into cavity, then apply pressure.
  • Tourniquet: Life-threatening limb bleed only. Apply 2-3" above wound, tighten until bleeding stops. Note time applied.
  • Improvised tourniquet: Wide band (belt, cloth) + windlass (stick). Never use wire/cord.
  • Pressure points: Brachial (inner arm), femoral (groin) as adjuncts, not replacements.
  • Clean wounds: Irrigate with clean water under pressure. Don't scrub.
  • Infection signs: Red streaking, pus, fever, increasing pain—evacuate.

Fractures & Sprains

  • Fracture vs sprain: Fracture = bone break (deformity, grinding, inability to bear weight). Sprain = ligament stretch (swelling, bruising, pain with movement).
  • Splinting: Immobilize joint above AND below injury. Use sticks, trekking poles, sleeping pad. Pad bony prominences.
  • Open fracture: Never push bone back in. Cover with sterile dressing, splint, evacuate.
  • Walk out vs wait: If stable, can bear weight, no neurovascular compromise → self-evacuate. Otherwise, signal for rescue.
  • RICE for sprains: Rest, Ice/Cool (stream, snow), Compression (elastic bandage), Elevation.

Hypothermia

  • Mild (shivering, alert): Remove wet layers, insulate, warm sweet drink if conscious.
  • Moderate (confusion, slurred speech): Handle gently, insulate head/neck, vapor barrier (plastic) under insulation.
  • Severe (no shivering, paradoxical undressing): Passive rewarming only—insulate, don't rub extremities. Evacuate immediately.
  • Afterdrop danger: Rewarming extremities first sends cold blood to core → cardiac risk. Rewarm core first.

Heat Stroke vs Exhaustion

  • Exhaustion: Sweating, pale, dizzy, nauseated. Treat: shade, rest, hydrate, cool.
  • Stroke (EMERGENCY): Hot dry skin OR profuse sweating, confusion, loss of consciousness. Treat: AGGRESSIVE COOLING—wet skin, fan, ice to neck/groin/armpits. Evacuate immediately.

Snake Bite

  • Stay calm, immobilize limb at heart level.
  • Mark swelling edge with pen, note time every 15min.
  • Remove rings/watches near bite.
  • DO NOT: cut/suck, tourniquet, ice, alcohol, electric shock.
  • Get to hospital—antivenin is only effective treatment.
  • Remember snake appearance (don't chase/kill).
  • Pressure immobilization for neurotoxic snakes (Australia, mambas): wrap entire limb firmly, splint. NOT for pit vipers (rattlesnakes).

Wound Care

  • Irrigation: Clean water under pressure (bag with pinhole).
  • Closure: Butterfly strips for clean cuts; leave punctures open.
  • Dressing: Non-stick pad + gauze + tape. Change daily, inspect for infection.
  • Infection red flags: Red streaks moving up limb (cellulitis), pus, fever, worsening pain—evacuate.

Altitude Sickness

  • AMS (above 8000ft): Headache, nausea, fatigue. Treat: rest, hydrate, don't ascend.
  • HAPE/HACE (fluid in lungs/brain): Cough, confusion, ataxia. DESCEND IMMEDIATELY—these kill.
  • Golden rule: Never ascend with symptoms. Acclimatize: ascend slowly, rest days.

Eye Injuries

  • Foreign body: Don't rub. Flush with clean water. If embedded, shield eye, evacuate.
  • Snow blindness: Pain, tearing, gritty feeling. Rest in dark, cool compresses. Prevention: improvised sunglasses (bark/cardboard with narrow slits).

Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis

  • Recognize: Hives, swelling throat/lips, difficulty breathing, dizziness.
  • Epinephrine: Use auto-injector if available (thigh, hold 10sec).
  • Position: Sitting if breathing issues; lying down with legs elevated if faint/low BP.
  • Evacuate: Even if symptoms improve—biphasic reaction possible.

Reading the Sky

Weather matters more in the field. A storm can turn a minor issue into a life-threatening situation.

Lightning Safety

30-30 Rule: If flash-to-thunder ≤30 seconds (<6 miles), shelter immediately. Wait 30 minutes after last thunder before resuming.

If caught in open: Crouch low, feet together, minimize ground contact. Avoid trees, ridges, caves, water. Remove metal frame pack.

Cloud Types & Meaning

  • Cumulus (puffy, cotton): Fair weather if small/scattered.
  • Cumulonimbus (anvil top, towering): Thunderstorm imminent—seek shelter.
  • Cirrus (wispy, high): Weather change in 24-48 hours.
  • Stratus (flat, grey layer): Persistent rain/drizzle likely.
  • Altocumulus ("mackerel sky"): Thunderstorms possible by afternoon.
  • Lenticular (lens over mountain): Extremely high winds aloft—avoid ridges.

Wind Signs

  • Increasing wind = increasing storm intensity.
  • Backing (counterclockwise shift): Worsening weather (N hemisphere).
  • Veering (clockwise shift): Improving weather.
  • Read wind: Wet finger (cool side = wind direction), throw grass/dirt.

Pressure Signs (No Barometer)

  • Aching joints/old injuries = dropping pressure (storm coming).
  • Campfire smoke swirling/descending = low pressure.
  • Sounds travel farther = high humidity, low pressure.
  • Stronger vegetation smell = humidity/rain approaching.

Animal Behavior

  • Cattle/sheep lying down = rain coming (avoid wet grass).
  • Swallows flying low = insects low due to pressure drop.
  • Frogs louder = high humidity, rain imminent.
  • Ants building mound walls = rain coming.
  • Bees staying near hive = storm imminent.

Red Sky Rules

  • "Red sky at night, shepherd's delight": High pressure moving in from west = fair weather.
  • "Red sky in morning, shepherd's warning": Moisture in eastern sky, system moving in = storm likely.

Temperature & Dew Point

Dew forms when temperature drops to dew point. Heavy morning dew = fair day likely (clear night = no cloud insulation). Frost risk: if night is clear/calm, temperature can drop 10-20°F below evening reading.

Practical Tips

  • Clean panels daily in dusty conditions—massive efficiency difference.
  • Partial shade on one cell affects whole panel—avoid shadows.
  • Morning frost: Wait for melt; don't wipe (scratches coating).
  • Angle matters: 10° off optimal = 5-15% loss depending on season.
  • Wiring: Series = higher voltage; parallel = higher current. Match to charge controller.
  • "100W panel" reality: Rated at STC (25°C, 1000W/m²). Real-world: 70-85W typical due to angle, temperature, losses.
